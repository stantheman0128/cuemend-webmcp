# 100-Idea Tournament

Status: active. This file records the evidence trail from 100 raw ideas to one build choice. Scores are internal strategy estimates, not official scores or guarantees.

## Tournament rules

### Gate 0 — eligibility and scope

Eliminate any idea that cannot credibly satisfy all of these within the remaining window:

- A deployed page exposes real WebMCP tools.
- A real agent completes a non-trivial multi-tool flow.
- The human and agent operate the same visible state.
- The project has a coherent end-to-end product experience.
- The live URL, public licensed repository, and public under-three-minute YouTube demo can all be completed and frozen before the deadline.
- No core claim depends on an unverified external API, specialized hardware, real money, real health/legal decisions, or scientific accuracy beyond the disclosed synthetic model.

### Round 1 — independent Top 16 ballots

Four independent judges score the complete [100-idea ledger](idea-ledger.md):

1. Official rubric and Stage 1 fit.
2. Collision/novelty against the observed field.
3. Brutal delivery feasibility from an almost-empty folder under current resource constraints.
4. Ten-second cold open, sixty-second human-agent loop, and product coherence.

Each returns a Top 16. Consensus and high upside wildcards advance; a single optimistic score cannot hide a hard gate or direct competitor collision.

### Round 2 — red-team 8

For each quarterfinalist, independent advocates and critics must answer:

1. What core capability disappears without WebMCP?
2. What exact tool call is visible inside the first 15 seconds?
3. What does the human know or decide that the agent cannot?
4. What does the agent exhaustively compute that the human should not do manually?
5. What objective replay, certificate, invariant, or artifact proves the result?
6. Which existing submission is closest, and what mechanism remains different?
7. What is the first fatal implementation risk?
8. Can one complete path be implemented, deployed, tested three times, filmed, and documented before the cutoff?

### Round 3 — semifinal 4

The semifinal jury uses 25 points per official criterion, enforces a minimum of 20 for WebMCP Leverage and 19 for Execution, and applies the official lexicographic tie-break only when totals are close.

### Final — one commitment

The winner must have:

- one sentence naming a concrete audience and pain;
- one 10-second signature transformation;
- 4–7 orthogonal, state-aware tools;
- at least one dynamic capability change;
- one failure/stale/adversarial path;
- one app-enforced human-only decision;
- one objective post-state proof;
- a zero-login deterministic demo and ordinary UI fallback.

Once selected, the topic is frozen. Delivery quality beats adding secondary features.

### Advancement rule applied

I converted each Top 16 into descending Borda points (16 for first through 1
for sixteenth), then treated the result only as a shortlist—not as the final
answer. Candidates also needed either support from two independent briefs or a
single exceptional novelty result with a demonstrable WebMCP-native mechanic.

The eight quarterfinalists are:

1. **CueKind** — best demo/product choice; strong official and novelty support.
2. **Flightpath** — best novelty choice; strong official support and instant visual proof.
3. **ZineFold** — four-ballot consensus and a very safe deterministic build.
4. **Focus Treaty** — strongest official fit and near-best delivery confidence.
5. **ShardWise** — novelty wildcard with a genuinely human conservation decision.
6. **ConsentCut Media** — strong visible revocation/recomposition loop, but a busy editor category.
7. **ChoreoGraph** — three-ballot animated constraint engine, tempered by ethical/domain risk.
8. **Field Evidence Time Machine** — novelty wildcard whose contradiction witness is distinct from generic science explorers.

Proof Garden, Cascade Court, Repair Relay and the remaining high scorers are cut:
each either collided directly with an inspected project, lacked a strong visual
product moment, or offered a smaller ceiling than one of the eight above.

## Quarterfinals

The eight advance into a stripped-domain state-machine debate. Advocates and
critics must compare the exact human-only input, exhaustive agent computation,
falsifiable invariant, visible shared-state transition and durable artifact.
No points are awarded merely for previews, approvals, receipts, undo or dynamic
tool registration; the inspected field already treats those as table stakes.

Because the resource governor remained RED, no new judge sessions were started.
Instead, the four already-completed reports were cross-examined against the
uniform state-machine brief in [`round2-brief.md`](round2-brief.md). This avoids
turning resource pressure into either an unreviewed choice or a missed deadline.

### Why CueMend beats the other seven

- **Counterfactual:** without WebMCP, an agent gets pixels or a caption-file blob;
  it cannot reliably share exact cue IDs, locked dramatic beats, current
  revision, staged alternatives and live playback state with the editor. With
  WebMCP, the site owns those semantics and enforces every mutation.
- **Human contribution:** the stage manager decides what laughter, silence,
  reveal and performance timing must not move. That is artistic authority, not
  an approval click.
- **Agent contribution:** the agent audits every interval and searches hundreds
  of bounded cue combinations. That is tedious and error-prone by hand, but
  deterministic and inspectable in the app.
- **Proof:** seven fixture-derived conflicts become zero, the locked cue digests
  remain identical, and the exported WebVTT re-audits cleanly under the exact
  named production profile.
- **Visible state:** the normal stage preview and timeline—not a developer log—
  show the before, staged A/B, human lock, replan and final verified track.
- **Artifact:** the VTT and certificate persist outside chat and remain editable.
- **Delivery:** it is one static, zero-login, zero-API app with a bounded search
  engine. The visual work is concentrated in one 32-second scene.

Featherline wins pure novelty, but loses the official tie-break order: the
scientific-surrogate disclaimer weakens impact and execution confidence before
creativity is considered. Focus Treaty is the cleanest engineering proof but
enters an already strong accessibility-testing lane. Field Evidence is novel but
requires more exposition before its payoff is legible.

## Final decision

**Build CueMend: “Repair the timing. Keep the beat.”**

CueMend is a rehearsal-room caption timing workbench for small theatres,
schools and live-event teams. A human protects the exact moments that carry the
performance; an agent exhaustively repairs the rest through state-aware WebMCP
tools; the app proves the result and exports editable WebVTT.

The original candidate name `CueKind` is retired because a live family-routine
service already uses it. A targeted web and GitHub check found no relevant
caption software or repository named `CueMend` at decision time. The topic and
name are now frozen for delivery.

### Frozen MVP

- One fictional, rights-safe 32-second performance with exactly seven initial
  cue conflicts and three human-locked artistic beats.
- A declared, configurable demo profile—not universal compliance claims.
- A real bounded-search engine over authored cue variants; no external model/API.
- Seven state-aware tools across base, proposed, approved and committed phases.
- Exact revision/digest binding, one stale rejection, a human-only approval
  control, one-shot commit authority and idempotent command handling.
- A/B stage playback, timeline, visible conflict explanations and activity rail.
- Post-commit re-audit, WebVTT export and a digest-bound certificate.
- Zero-login reset, ordinary UI fallback, native WebMCP evidence, focused tests,
  public deployment/repository and an under-three-minute narrated demo package.

Everything else—uploads, accounts, persistence, translation, live speech,
automatic text rewriting, multiple productions and generalized standards—is
explicitly out of scope.
